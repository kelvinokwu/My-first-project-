01 — The tube

The GI tract, part by part

One continuous tube, mouth to anus. Each part has one main job. If a question describes a function, it's really just asking "which organ is this."

Mouth mechanical + carbs

Chews food, mixes with saliva. Salivary amylase starts breaking starch. Tongue rolls it into a bolus.

Pharynx traffic cop

Shared with the airway. Just moves the bolus through and makes sure it doesn't go down the wrong pipe.

Oesophagus delivery only

No digestion here. Peristalsis pushes food down. Lower oesophageal sphincter keeps stomach acid from coming back up.

Stomach protein starts here

Parietal cells: HCl + intrinsic factor. Chief cells: pepsinogen + gastric lipase. Mucous cells: protective mucus. HCl turns pepsinogen into pepsin → early protein digestion. Food leaves as chyme.

Duodenum the mixing bowl

Acidic chyme + bile (from liver/gallbladder) + pancreatic juice. Bicarbonate neutralizes acid. Most chemical digestion kicks off here.

Jejunum absorption MVP

Villi and microvilli everywhere. Main site for absorbing digested carbs, proteins, fats, water, electrolytes, vitamins.

Ileum the specialist

Finishes absorption. Special job: vitamin B12 (with intrinsic factor) and bile salts, which get recycled back to the liver.

Large intestine the dehydrator

No villi. Absorbs water + electrolytes, hosts the microbiota, turns leftovers into faeces.

Accessory organs

Liver makes bile. Gallbladder stores/concentrates it. Pancreas is both endocrine and exocrine — exocrine side sends enzymes + bicarbonate to the duodenum.

High-yield: if a question says "neutralizes acid" → duodenum / pancreatic bicarbonate. If it says "emulsifies fat" → bile salts, not an enzyme. Bile salts don't digest anything — they just break fat into smaller droplets.
02 — Breaking it down

Carbs, protein, fat

Same pattern for all three: a little starts in the mouth/stomach, most of the real work happens in the small intestine.

NutrientMouthStomachSmall intestineFinal products
Carbs Salivary amylase → starch breakdown begins Amylase inactivated by acid; little digestion happens Pancreatic amylase → maltose/oligosaccharides; brush-border enzymes (maltase, sucrase, lactase) finish the job Glucose, galactose, fructose
Protein None (mechanical only) HCl activates pepsinogen → pepsin; proteins → peptides Trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidases (pancreas) + brush-border peptidases Amino acids, dipeptides, tripeptides
Fat Lingual lipase (minor) Gastric lipase (minor) Bile salts emulsify → pancreatic lipase digests → micelles carry products to epithelium Fatty acids + monoglycerides → reassembled into triglycerides → chylomicrons
Key sequence
Big fat globs can't just float into the blood — bile salts chop them up, lipase digests the pieces, micelles taxi them to the intestinal wall, and inside the cell they get repackaged as chylomicrons, which skip the blood entirely and go into the lacteals (lymph) first. Short/medium-chain fatty acids don't bother with any of this — straight into portal blood.
Absorption route: sugars and amino acids → blood → hepatic portal vein → liver. Long-chain fats → lymphatic lacteals → bloodstream (not the portal vein first). This distinction gets tested constantly.
03 — When it breaks

Two GI conditions to know cold

Dysphagia swallowing
Difficulty or discomfort swallowing. Two flavors:
  • Oropharyngeal — trouble starting the swallow / moving food from mouth+pharynx into the oesophagus. Usually a muscle or nerve problem.
  • Oesophageal — food struggles to get through the oesophagus itself. Causes: strictures, tumours, inflammation, motility disorders, GERD.
Peptic ulcer acid damage
Open sore in the stomach or duodenal lining. Happens when acid/pepsin damage > mucosal protection.
  • Top cause: H. pylori infection
  • Second cause: long-term NSAID use (aspirin, ibuprofen) — these cut protective prostaglandins, which lowers mucus + bicarbonate secretion and reduces mucosal blood flow
  • Normal defense = mucus + bicarbonate layer + intact epithelium + good blood flow. Weaken any of these and acid wins.
04 — The "master gland"

Pituitary gland

Sits in the sella turcica below the hypothalamus, right next to the optic chiasma (tumour → visual problems). Two functionally different halves.

Anterior pituitary makes hormones

Glandular tissue. Controlled by hypothalamic releasing/inhibiting hormones via the hypophyseal portal system. Makes: GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, prolactin.

Posterior pituitary stores hormones

Nervous tissue — makes nothing itself. Just stores + releases ADH and oxytocin, which are actually made by hypothalamic neurons.

GH

Growth of bone/muscle via IGF-1 (liver). Also raises blood glucose + promotes fat breakdown. Excess in kids → gigantism; in adults → acromegaly.

TSH

Drives thyroid to make T3/T4. Controlled by hypothalamic TRH, shut off by thyroid hormone feedback.

ACTH

Drives adrenal cortex to make cortisol. Excess from a pituitary tumour = Cushing disease.

FSH

Females: grows ovarian follicles + estrogen. Males: supports Sertoli cells → spermatogenesis.

LH

Females: LH surge triggers ovulation, then builds corpus luteum. Males: drives Leydig cells → testosterone.

Prolactin

Milk production. Unusual — mainly inhibited by hypothalamic dopamine. Suckling ↓ dopamine → ↑ prolactin.

ADH (vasopressin)

↑ water reabsorption at kidney collecting ducts. High doses vasoconstrict. Deficiency → diabetes insipidus.

Oxytocin

Uterine contraction in labour (positive feedback) + milk ejection (let-down reflex). Not the same as prolactin — prolactin makes milk, oxytocin pushes it out.

MSH (pars intermedia)

Melanin production. Big in some animals, minor player in humans.

Don't mix these up: prolactin makes milk; oxytocin ejects milk.
05 — Nine months of hormones

Hormones of pregnancy

Implantation→ hCG→ corpus luteum kept alive→ progesterone→ endometrium maintained

hCG

First hormone detectable — this is what pregnancy tests pick up. Made by the syncytiotrophoblast. Job: keep the corpus luteum alive so it keeps making progesterone.

Progesterone

Corpus luteum early → placenta later. Maintains endometrium, relaxes uterine muscle (prevents early labour), preps breasts, dampens maternal immune response to the fetus.

Estrogen

Mostly placental. Grows the uterus, develops mammary ducts, and near term increases oxytocin receptors + prostaglandins to prep for labour.

hPL

↑ maternal insulin resistance → spares glucose for the fetus. Also breaks down maternal fat for energy. This is the hormone behind gestational diabetes risk.

Relaxin

Loosens pelvic ligaments + softens cervix. Reduces uterine contractility alongside progesterone.

Oxytocin

Labour contractions (positive feedback loop) + milk ejection after birth.

Placental CRH

Rises through pregnancy (opposite of normal CRH feedback). Drives ACTH/cortisol and is linked to the timing of labour onset.

Cortisol

Maternal metabolism support + matures fetal lungs before birth.

Why milk doesn't leak during pregnancy: prolactin is high, but estrogen + progesterone block its milk-making effect. Delivery → placenta gone → estrogen/progesterone crash → prolactin finally works unopposed.
06 — Making sperm

Spermatogenesis

Happens in the seminiferous tubules. Takes ~2–2.5 months. Cells mature as they move from the basement membrane toward the lumen.

Spermatogonium (2n)→ Primary spermatocyte (2n)→ meiosis I → 2 Secondary spermatocytes (n)→ meiosis II → 4 Spermatids (n)→ spermiogenesis → Spermatozoa

Sertoli cells support cells

Support/nourish germ cells, form the blood-testis barrier, secrete androgen-binding protein (keeps local testosterone high) and inhibin B (dials FSH down). Respond to FSH.

Leydig cells the factory

Sit between the tubules. Make testosterone in response to LH.

Hypothalamus (GnRH)→ Anterior pituitary→ LH → Leydig cells → testosterone | FSH → Sertoli cells → supports spermatogenesis
Spermiogenesis, fast
Golgi → acrosome (fertilization enzymes). Nucleus → condenses (small streamlined head). Centriole → flagellum (movement). Mitochondria → middle piece (ATP for the tail). Extra cytoplasm gets dumped as residual bodies. End result: a haploid cell built for one job — swim and deliver DNA.
Sperm are structurally finished after spermiogenesis but aren't functionally ready — they mature and gain motility in the epididymis, not the testis.
07 — Kidney as an endocrine organ

Endocrine kidney

Renin

Released by juxtaglomerular cells when renal blood flow/BP drops. Starts RAAS: angiotensinogen → angiotensin I → (ACE) → angiotensin II → vasoconstriction + aldosterone release → Na⁺ and water retention → BP restored.

Erythropoietin

Made when kidney oxygen levels fall. Tells bone marrow to make more red blood cells. Explains why chronic kidney disease → anaemia.

Kidney also activates vitamin D (calcitriol), feeding into calcium regulation — worth remembering as a third endocrine job even though it's covered lightly here.

10 — Summary

Final recap

• Most digestion and all absorption happens in the small intestine, not the stomach.
• Bile salts emulsify fat, they don't digest it — pancreatic lipase does the digesting.
• Anterior pituitary makes hormones (GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, prolactin). Posterior pituitary just stores/releases ADH and oxytocin.
• Prolactin makes milk. Oxytocin ejects it. Different jobs, don't swap them.
• hCG keeps the corpus luteum alive early on; the placenta takes over hormone production later.
• LH → Leydig cells → testosterone. FSH → Sertoli cells → supports sperm development.
• Renin starts RAAS when blood pressure or renal blood flow drops.
• Type 1 diabetes = no insulin. Type 2 = insulin resistance first.
